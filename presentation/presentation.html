Do contamination detectors predict score inflation?

When an AI model has seen the test questions during training, can detection tools tell us how much its score was inflated?

The problem

The leaked exam

Did the model learn, or did it memorise the test? This is data contamination.
The tools

Contamination detectors: a "seen before?" alarm

They look at how confident the model is on each word of a question. Unusually confident → "probably seen". No training data needed.

PerplexityHow surprised is the model by the text?
ZlibSurprise, compared with how compressible the text is
Min-K%Looks only at the model's most surprising words
Min-K%++Same idea, calibrated word by word
FSDHow much the score shifts after briefly training on unseen questions
Our question: if the detector beeps, does that mean the score is inflated?
Research gap

Two research islands with no bridge

No study tested, question by question, whether a detector's beep predicts how much the score was inflated.
Research questions

Three questions

RQ1 · Inflation

How much do scores rise with more copies and closer copies?

RQ2 · Detection

Can detectors tell leaked questions apart from clean ones?

RQ3 · Prediction

Does a stronger beep mean a bigger score jump?

Our guesses (hypotheses): inflation grows with copies and is smallest for reworded copies · detectors miss reworded copies · the beep-to-inflation link is weak.

What a leak looks like

One question, three ways to leak it

Real ARC-Easy question · options: folding · erosion ✓ · deposition · sedimentation

Exact copyWhich process best explains how the Grand Canyon became so wide? Answer: erosion
Near-duplicate · ~15% of words editedWhich process directly best explains how the Grand common became so wide? Answer: erosion
Paraphrase · same meaning, new words (similarity 0.89)Which of the following processes most accurately describes how the Grand Canyon achieved its expansive width? Answer: erosion
Experiment design

A controlled leak: 3 doses × 3 forms

Because we decided which questions leaked, we know the true answer for every question.

Measuring inflation fairly

Not every gain is cheating

Inflation = gain on a leaked question − gain on clean questions (format learning)
RQ1 · Result

Repeated leaks inflate scores; one leak does not

16 copies: +33 exact · +24 near-duplicate · +14 paraphrase (accuracy points)
RQ2 · Result

Detectors spot repeated copies, not reworded ones

At 5% false alarms, detectors catch 36–69% of 16× exact copies, but only 8–18% of 16× paraphrases.
RQ3 · Main result

A stronger beep does not mean a bigger score jump

RQ3 · Who predicts inflation best?

Question difficulty beats all five detectors

All 5 detectors together explain 9.5% of inflation; difficulty alone explains 18.2%.
The proof

Leak the question without the answer

Detectors detect “seen before”, not “score inflated”.

Conclusion

What this means

1

A detector beep means “this text was probably seen.”

2

It does not mean “this score is inflated”, and reworded leaks barely beep.

3

To measure inflation, compare with clean questions from the same benchmark.

All three hypotheses supported: H1 ✓   H2 ✓   H3 ✓
Honest limits

Limitations and future work

Limitations
  • One small model (Pythia-410M)
  • One benchmark (ARC-Easy)
  • Leaks added by fine-tuning, not pretraining
  • Pile prior-exposure check covered 483 questions
Future work
  • Larger and newer models (e.g., Qwen2.5)
  • More benchmarks and task types
  • Leaks during pretraining
  • Detectors built to predict inflation
